Pad Thai

The wok is screaming, the clock is running, and dinner takes nine minutes.

Pad Thai as the world knows it was a 1930s government campaign — a national dish designed to unify Thailand and promote rice-noodle consumption. It worked suspiciously well. The street version is a study in rhythm: tamarind, fish sauce and palm sugar pre-mixed, noodles soaked soft, and then ninety seconds of violent, joyful wok work.

The home version lives or dies on preparation. Once the pan is hot there is no time to measure anything — everything must be at arm's reach, chopped, soaked, cracked. Cook one serving at a time if your pan is small; a crowded wok steams instead of frying, and steamed pad thai is just wet noodles with regret.

✦
Kitchen wisdom. The 1930s promotion was driven by Prime Minister Plaek Phibunsongkhram's nationalism — and a government fear that rice exports were outpacing domestic rice consumption. Noodles as economic policy.

Method

  1. 1

    Mix the sauce, soak the noodles

    Stir tamarind, fish sauce and palm sugar until dissolved — taste it: sour-salty-sweet, provocative, delicious. Soak noodles until they bend without snapping; they finish cooking in the wok.

  2. 2

    Scorching hot, everything ready

    Get your widest pan or wok properly hot. Oil. Garlic and shallots in, 20 seconds — aromatic, not brown. Protein next, seared hard.

  3. 3

    Noodles and sauce

    Add drained noodles and the sauce. Toss continuously for 2 minutes. If the pan is dry, add a splash of water; the noodles steam-tenderize while the sauce reduces and glazes them.

  4. 4

    The egg curtain

    Push everything to one side, crack the eggs into the empty half, scramble for 20 seconds until half-set, then fold through the noodles. Some cooks wrap the noodles in the egg like a blanket; be that cook.

  5. 5

    Finish off the heat

    Tofu, sprouts and chives in, toss once, off the pan — sprouts should stay crisp. Peanuts, lime squeeze, chili flakes on top. The best pad thai is sweet-sour-salty in that order and eaten standing at the stove.

Chef's notes

  • Tamarind concentrate from an Asian grocery is the flavor backbone. Lime juice is an acceptable improvisation, but it is a different accent.
  • One serving per pan. Seriously. The dish takes nine minutes; cook two rounds.
  • Soak, never boil, the noodles — boiled rice noodles turn to paste in the wok.
Keeping it: Best eaten within minutes. The sauce base keeps 3 weeks refrigerated — make a jar, and pad thai becomes a 10-minute weeknight habit.
